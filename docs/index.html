Ministers of the Italian Republic (1946-2026)

An analysis of the bridges between wikidata and reality

Research Agenda

The here presented data analysis is the final draft of months of work for the module "From data to knowledge: introduction to data analysis using Linked Open Data" of the DH department. The first step was to choose a population that could be defined in wikipedia and then to aks ourselves interesting questions that could lead to insights. The reasons bedhind my choice of Italian Ministers of the Republic are mainly about the close relationship that there is between the political class of a country and its citizens. So the research questions develop around evolution in time of certain aspects like percentages of gender, birth place, cultural background...
ID Focus dell'Analisi Domanda di Ricerca (Research Question)
RQ1 Genere e Affiliazione Politica Can a connection be seen between the Prime Minister's party affiliation and the percentages of gender representation inside the government?
RQ2 Background Culturale (Prima vs Seconda Repubblica) Is there a radical change that can be traced in the cultural background of government members between the First and Second Republics?
RQ3 Origine Geografica (Nord vs Sud) Has there been an evolution in the percentages of ministers' regions of origin between North and South through time?
RQ4 Formazione all'Estero Has there been an increase in abroad training experiences in the cultural background of ministers through time?
As we'll see during the presentation none of these Research Questions will actually find an answer; the reasons are mainly because of the quality of data that can be find in wikidata, that are not fully suitable for this type of research. The small number of individuals (around 570) and the main property chosen as identifier, occupation as minsiters, make the results of the analysis byasesd under multiple aspects; as the periods of activity, that can't be fully traced because properties such as the singular governments are missing in wikidata.
